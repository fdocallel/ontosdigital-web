Case · Organisation

Every project with its history and its next step

How a digital twin puts projects, tasks, calendar and contacts in order, and answers in plain language the question every Monday brings: where does each thing stand?

Watch the animated demo → about 1 minute · no sound · sample data

The problem

A small business does not usually have a work problem: it has a status problem. Every open project lives scattered between somebody's head, a folder of emails, a spreadsheet nobody updates and a messaging group. Nobody can answer the simple question quickly: what is next on each front?

The cost never shows up on an invoice. It is paid in deadlines discovered late, in calls to reconstruct what was already discussed, and in decisions postponed because putting the information in order costs more than taking them.

What was built

A digital twin: a data model where every fact lives exactly once, and an AI that operates it daily. For organisation, three concrete pieces:

Projects with a next step. Each project is a record with its timeline of milestones, its current phase and a single line answering what matters: what the next move is and who it depends on.

Tasks and calendar, with no duplication. Tasks hold the what; the calendar holds the when: appointments and hard deadlines. Each thing lives in one place only and the two views stay in sync, so no list goes stale.

Contacts with context. Every person who appears in a project has their record: what happened last with them and what comes next. Before a call, the context is ready in seconds.

You ask questions of that model in plain language, and the answer comes back ordered and with its sources: which record each piece of data comes from.

How it works day to day

The demo tells it through a staged business (sample data, not real): three open projects. A shop refurbishment on site, with its timeline of milestones and the next step in plain view: the surveyor's visit. An opening licence in progress, with a request for further information to answer. And a new website at proposal stage, waiting on a choice of quote.

On one side, the tasks: send the documents to the bank, get quotes for signage, renew the insurance. On the other, the calendar: the surveyor's visit on Tuesday, the accountant on Thursday, and the deadline for the request on Friday. Nothing is in two places at once, so ticking a task off is enough for the whole system to know.

The contact network keeps the thread of each relationship: the architect on the refurbishment, with the drawings already received and the finishes still to confirm; the web studio, with the proposal sent and an answer expected on Thursday; the accountant, with the response under way (people and dates staged).

And the closing scene is the one that matters: asking “what is on this week?” and getting the answer back ordered, with Friday's deadline in the foreground and every statement linked to its record. That is what AI does when the data is properly modelled: it does not search, it consults.

Result

I built the first digital twin for myself, and I keep it up to date daily with AI since June 2026. Today, in the organisation area:

  • Projects with their next step always in view; tasks and calendar in sync; contacts up to date.
  • More than 400 log entries and more than 300 documents searchable in plain language.
  • An automatic audit checks every day that nothing is lost.
  • All 100% local and private.
